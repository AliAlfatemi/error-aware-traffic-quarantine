# Causal Selector → Real HTB Experiment Protocol

Status: frozen before the authoritative campaign on 2026-09-13. This protocol
is prospectively frozen but is not an independently registered protocol.

## Question and evidence boundary

This experiment tests whether scheduler conclusions survive when the synthetic
20-IAT selector is connected causally to a real Linux HTB queue. Selector
decisions are computed in userspace and replayed by choosing one of two
pre-marked UDP sockets; Linux `tc` performs the TOS-to-class mapping and all
packet service and drops occur in a real HTB/bfifo hierarchy on a disposable
veth pair.

This is evidence for a rootless, single-host veth testbed with userspace causal
decision replay. It is not XDP/eBPF execution, a physical-link test, a
line-rate result, or a measurement of classifier compute cost in a forwarding
hook.

## Pre-authoritative engineering history

Three non-evidentiary smoke trees were created before this freeze and are
excluded from every estimate and test.

- `smoke_v1` established process and qdisc operation, but its 48 flows per
  family did not load HTB and its exact-zero paired send-count rule rejected a
  one-packet difference among 22,835 planned packets.
- Before `smoke_v2`, the held-out population was increased to 192 independently
  generated flows per family and the paired send-count tolerance was replaced
  by the pre-existing 0.5% relative fidelity gate.
- `smoke_v2` activated shaping and exposed IPv4 fragmentation for generated UDP
  payloads above 1,472 bytes.
- Before `smoke_v3`, both veth endpoints were fixed at MTU 9,000 and fail-closed
  sender → filter → leaf qdisc → receiver packet-conservation checks were added.
  `smoke_v3` passed those checks. No smoke endpoint enters authoritative
  analysis.

These amendments were engineering corrections made before the protocol and
authoritative output path were frozen. The final experiment configuration is
`configs/selector_htb.json`, SHA-256
`99588142dbf0bf0ceaae0a091080a0eca1e6fd1254ad6ce124aa4c92fc77399e`.

## Frozen population, selector, and packet rule

The existing coupled-simulation generator and selector fitting code are reused.
Training seeds and calibration seeds come only from
`configs/coupled_simulation.json`; no held-out seed is used to fit a coefficient
or threshold. The 30 frozen held-out seeds are the inference units.

For each held-out seed and attack scale 0.25, 1.0, or 4.0, the generator creates
192 flows per base family. Packet sizes, IATs, start times, labels, and family
mix are generated by the committed deterministic workload code. The expansion
in flow population raises offered load without changing packet sizes,
timestamps within a flow, labels, or selector features.

Two selectors are evaluated:

1. `multifeature`: the trained and calibration-thresholded model.
2. `oracle`: a maturity-matched synthetic upper boundary, not a deployable
   comparator.

Packet indices 0 through 19 of every flow are provisional FAST. Packet index 20
creates the twentieth IAT and may use the frozen decision. A flow that never
reaches maturity remains FAST. The complete replay trace and its SHA-256 digest
must be identical between scheduler arms of a pair.

## Frozen scheduler contrast and resources

The root service is 8,000,000 B/s. FAST reserves 70% (5,600,000 B/s) and the
suspicious class reserves 30% (2,400,000 B/s). Each child buffer is its reserved
rate multiplied by 20 ms; total child buffer is therefore 160,000 bytes in
both arms. Both children have HTB priority zero.

- `fixed`: each child ceiling equals its reservation.
- `borrowing`: both child ceilings equal the 8,000,000 B/s root rate.

Only the two child ceilings may differ within a pair. Root rate, reservations,
buffers, burst/cburst, priority, TOS filters, workload, sender rule, MTU, and
measurement interval remain identical.

The topology contains only loopback and one veth endpoint in each of two nested
network namespaces, has no default route, disables IPv6, and uses permanent
neighbor entries. Test traffic is limited to RFC 5737 TEST-NET-2. The runner
must refuse to execute outside the disposable outer network namespace.

## Design and execution

The factorial design contains 30 held-out seeds × 3 attack scales × 2 selectors
= 180 pairs and 360 arms. Pair order and within-pair arm order are randomized
once using execution seed 921731. Each trace lasts 12 seconds: the first 2
seconds are warm-up and the following 10 seconds are measured. A 2-second drain
follows traffic. Every output directory is create-only.

The authoritative campaign runs once on one compute host in a persistent
session. Host replications of the earlier localhost experiment are a separate
descriptive study and are never pooled with this campaign. GPU hardware is not
used because neither the deterministic selector nor HTB service has a CUDA
code path.

## Validity gates

An arm is invalid if any of the following occurs:

- more than 1% of deadlines are skipped or sent-packet p99 lateness exceeds
  2 ms;
- malformed packets, receive-buffer errors, namespace-local UDP errors, or
  socket overflow occur;
- live qdisc/class/filter state differs from the requested rates, ceilings,
  buffers, priorities, or TOS mapping;
- exact per-class conservation fails between sender counts, successful filter
  matches, leaf transmitted-plus-dropped packets, and receiver counts.

A pair also fails if its trace digests differ or its sent-packet counts differ
by more than 0.5%. Analysis is blocked unless all 360 arms and all 180 pairs
pass mechanically.

## Frozen endpoints and statistics

The paired effect is always `borrowing − fixed`. The 30 held-out seeds are
resampled as pairs. For every selector and attack scale, the analysis reports:

- benign payload goodput in B/s;
- benign one-way p99 latency in ms, recomputed by nearest rank from the retained
  lossless latency samples;
- attack payload delivered through FAST in B/s;
- total attack service in B/s; and
- total payload goodput in B/s.

The primary family is fixed before outcomes: `multifeature` at attack scale
4.0 for benign goodput, benign p99 latency, and attack FAST leakage. Each uses
the arithmetic mean paired effect, a 10,000-resample paired percentile 95% CI,
and an exact two-sided sign test with zeros excluded. The three p-values use
Holm familywise correction at alpha 0.05. Practical-reference magnitudes are
80,000 B/s for both rate endpoints and 5 ms for latency; statistical rejection
alone is not called practically important.

All other selector/scale/endpoints, including the oracle boundary, are
secondary descriptive estimates. No data-dependent endpoint, scale, seed,
direction, or multiplicity family may be substituted after the run.
